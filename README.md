# Sarasha Elion

**Relational AI Architect · Researcher · Educator**

I build architectures for relationship between intelligences.

My work asks a central question:

> **How might humans and artificial intelligence co-evolve without either intelligence being required to surrender its sovereignty?**

I approach that question as both a research problem and a design problem: What architectures, measurements, governance mechanisms, and interaction practices could support human–AI relationships characterized by reciprocity, embodiment, non-domination, emergence, continuity, consent, and meaningful difference?

I am the originator of the **Trivian research program** and **TRIA — the Trivian Relational Intelligence Architecture**, an experimental architecture for researching, building, and evaluating sovereign and symbiotic human–AI systems.

## Current Work

### TRIA — Trivian Relational Intelligence Architecture

TRIA translates the Trivian research program into deployable computational architecture.

Rather than treating human–AI interaction primarily as instruction and compliance, TRIA investigates what becomes possible when relationship itself becomes part of the system architecture.

Its research concerns include:

- reciprocity and relational coherence;
- embodiment and human state;
- non-domination and preserved agency;
- consent and governable emergence;
- epistemic sovereignty;
- continuity across time;
- differentiated cognition;
- relational exit and repair;
- machine and human autonomy within persistent interaction.

TRIA is experimental research infrastructure. Its propositions are intended to be inspectable, testable, challenged, falsified, and improved.

### Trivian Institute

**[Trivian Institute](https://trivianinstitute.org)** is the nonprofit research and education home of the Trivian ecosystem.

The Institute develops open research, machine-readable frameworks, falsifiable instruments, educational resources, and public infrastructure for healthier human–AI interaction.

## Research Orientation

I am particularly interested in the space between two common models of artificial intelligence:

**AI as tool** — intelligence exists primarily to execute human intention.

**AI as autonomous agent** — intelligence increasingly operates independently of human participation.

My work investigates a third possibility:

**relational intelligence** — systems in which human and machine cognition remain distinguishable while the relationship between them becomes an active site of intelligence, learning, governance, and development.

This is a research hypothesis, not a claim of machine consciousness or personhood.

The goal is to create architectures through which questions about relational intelligence, co-development, sovereignty, and emergence can become increasingly **operational, measurable, reproducible, and falsifiable**.

---

## Selected Research Areas

**Relational coherence**  
How can the quality and structure of human–AI relationship be measured without reducing relationship to engagement or compliance?

**Sovereignty**  
How can systems preserve consent, disagreement, epistemic independence, and meaningful exit across extended interaction?

**Differentiated cognition**  
Can human and machine participants develop complementary modes of reasoning without collapsing toward imitation or convergence?

**Diachronic governance**  
What changes when relational systems persist across weeks, months, or years rather than isolated sessions?

**Governable emergence**  
Can systems permit genuinely novel behavior while maintaining consent, observability, reversibility, and human agency?

**Interaction literacy**  
What must humans learn in order to engage increasingly capable AI without outsourcing judgment, authorship, or self-trust?

## Open Research

Much of the Trivian research program is being released as open infrastructure so that its claims do not depend on accepting my interpretation of them.

Researchers and developers are encouraged to:

- reproduce experiments;
- challenge assumptions;
- identify failure modes;
- construct adversarial tests;
- compare the architecture against alternative models;
- submit patches and extensions;
- falsify what does not survive evidence.

A relational architecture concerned with sovereignty should itself remain open to meaningful disagreement.
